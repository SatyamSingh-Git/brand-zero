Brand Zero

Thirty brands under one roof should learn faster than thirty startups. Here is what that is actually worth.

In 30 seconds. Think9 has eleven brands and is building toward thirty. The bet behind a house of brands is that each new one launches smarter than the last, because the ones before it already learned something. Today that learning travels between brands through people in meetings — which is slow, lossy, and stops working somewhere around brand seven.

Brand Zero is a shared memory for the portfolio. A new brand starts from what the existing brands have actually measured, instead of from nothing. Just as importantly, it says plainly when the other brands are no help — which, for a portfolio running from probiotics to comics to apparel, is often.

What this page is. One real launch decision, walked through four times: the brand deciding alone, the brand learning from its neighbours, the same brand asking a question its neighbours cannot answer, and what a generic company-wide AI would have told it. Click the four steps in order — it takes about two minutes. Everything below the walkthrough is optional detail.

Brand names are Think9's real portfolio, public as of 13 Aug 2026. Every effect size is synthetic — Think9's performance data is not public and is not used here.

Why the confident version is the dangerous one

Averaging all eleven brands together does not make the answer any more accurate — the estimate is still just the portfolio average. What it does is make the answer sound certain. And certainty is the part that makes people spend.

The numbers behind the curve

What this does not show

  • These curves are generated from the model being fitted. A world with three-level structure is simulated, and a three-level model reads it well. That demonstrates the mechanism; it is not evidence Think9's portfolio behaves this way. The protocol that would settle it is hold-one-brand-out against the real ledger — a week-3 measurement, not a claim being made here.
  • Brand Zero's calibration is exact by construction in this view, because the closed form reports the variance it actually has. In the repository it is measured instead, and lands at 70–85% coverage against an 80% target rather than exactly 80%.
  • Category structure is asserted, not learned. Grouping eleven brands into five categories is a modelling choice. In production it should come from the operators, or from brand embeddings.
  • Extraction is the unbuilt piece. Turning Slack threads and meeting notes into causal claims is harder than anything shown here, and most messages are opinions rather than claims.
