Brand Zero

If Brand 30 costs as much to get right as Brand 1, you don't have a house of brands. You have thirty startups sharing an office.

Brand names are Think9's real portfolio, public as of 13 Aug 2026. Every effect size here is synthetic — Think9's performance data is not public and is not used.

What a new brand inherits

A brand about to launch faces a decision — what creative hook, what price ladder — and has measured nothing. The question is how much its siblings can tell it before it spends anything. Move the sliders: these are the assumptions, and the case has to survive you changing them.

And what portfolio size buys — which is much less

Same question, one variable swapped: not how many siblings the new brand has, but how many brands Think9 owns in total. This is the number the pitch wanted to be big.

Both figures are variance reduction for a brand about to launch. The sibling number is computed live from the sliders above. The portfolio number is measured, by fitting the full model to simulated histories at each size and averaging over six worlds — the closed form driving this page treats the transfer structure as known, so it would understate it. Source: cold_start.py.

Why naive pooling is worse than building nothing

One pooled average over every brand — the shape most "central intelligence layer" proposals quietly take. It does not reduce error at all; its point estimate is still the portfolio mean. It only reduces the stated uncertainty, which is the part that gets people to act.

What this does not show

  • These curves are generated from the model being fitted. A world with three-level structure is simulated, and a three-level model reads it well. That demonstrates the mechanism; it is not evidence Think9's portfolio behaves this way. The protocol that would settle it is hold-one-brand-out against the real ledger — a week-3 measurement, not a claim being made here.
  • Brand Zero's calibration is exact by construction in this view, because the closed form reports the variance it actually has. In the repository it is measured instead, and it lands at 70–85% coverage against an 80% target rather than exactly 80%.
  • Category structure is asserted, not learned. Grouping eleven brands into five categories is a modelling choice made by whoever wrote the config. In production it should come from the operators, or from brand embeddings.
  • Extraction is the unbuilt piece. Turning Slack threads and meeting notes into causal claims is harder than anything shown here, and most messages are opinions rather than claims. The evidence tiers are the design response, not a solution.
