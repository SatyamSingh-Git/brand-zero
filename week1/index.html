Brand Zero · Week 1

The part that runs on Monday.

Everything else in this proposal needs sibling brands before it pays. This does not. It needs one thing: that before a launch decision, the person making it writes down what they expect — and gets scored afterwards. Two quarters of that tells Think9 which operators are calibrated on which kinds of decision, which is worth having whether or not the rest of the system is ever built.

This page is the whole week-one tool. One file, no install, no database, no engineering time. It saves to your browser and exports to CSV or JSON in the exact schema the model reads.

Nothing here is pre-filled with Think9 data. The brand list is Think9's real public portfolio; the ledger starts empty apart from three rows marked example that show the shape. Any real entry is one you type.

Before the decision

Ninety seconds, before the launch or the test. The point is not to be right — it is to be on record, so that later you can find out whether you were. Give the range you would genuinely be surprised to fall outside.

One sentence. Specific enough that in six weeks you can tell whether it happened.
Percent change you expect. Negative is allowed.
You should be wrong about one time in five. The range you give is an 80% range: if the truth lands inside it every single time, your ranges are too wide to be useful. If it lands inside less than half the time, they are too narrow to be safe. Both are fixable, and neither is visible until someone writes the ranges down.

Backfill a decision you already made

The most valuable day of week one, and it involves no AI at all: someone who was in the room writes down twenty to thirty past decisions. This is the form for that. Be honest about the last field — it is the one that decides how much this claim is allowed to move anything.

Context is what stops this being pooled with something it has nothing to do with.
Required. A claim nobody can trace is a rumour, and the schema rejects it.

After the outcome is known

Come back when the result is in. Enter what actually happened and every forecast recorded against that decision is scored at once — including, eventually, the model's.

The ledger

Everything recorded so far. Export lands in the same schema brandzero/schema.py reads, so week two's pipeline can pick it up without a migration.

Who is calibrated

Scored by the Winkler interval score — the same rule brandzero/calibration.py applies to the model. Lower is better. It penalises wide ranges a little and confident misses a lot, so the way to win is to be honest rather than either bold or vague.

The number to read is coverage, not the score. Someone at 90% coverage is being careful; someone at 20% holds strong opinions too tightly. Neither is a bad operator — but only one of them knows it, and only after enough decisions to tell. Twenty is roughly where this starts being worth reading.
